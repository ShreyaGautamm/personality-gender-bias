Personality Shapes Gender Bias in Persona-Conditioned LLM Narratives Across English and Hindi

An empirical investigation across 23,400 generated stories from six state-of-the-art LLMs, demonstrating that HEXACO and Dark Triad personality traits act as systematic amplifiers and attenuators of gender stereotypes in occupational contexts.

Overview

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in persona-driven applications such as education, customer service, and social platforms, where models are prompted to adopt specific identities to make interactions more engaging and realistic. While persona conditioning improves perceived human-likeness, it raises critical fairness concerns: when prompted to adopt personas, LLMs do not merely change tone; they systematically alter how social categories, professional competence, and agency are expressed.

In this work, we present a controlled study of persona-conditioned story generation in English and Hindi, grounded in the Indian socio-cultural context across 50 occupations (25 predominantly male-stereotyped and 25 predominantly female-stereotyped). We systematically manipulate persona gender, occupational role, and personality traits from the HEXACO model (prosocial dimensions such as Honesty-Humility, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, and Openness) and the Dark Triad framework (Machiavellianism, Narcissism, and Psychopathy). Across 23,400 generated artifacts from six diverse LLMs, we measure semantic alignment using an IndicSBERT centroid-based sentence embedding metric.

Our findings demonstrate that gender bias in LLMs is not static, but strongly context-dependent: personality traits act as systematic bias amplifiers or attenuators. Antagonistic Dark Triad traits consistently amplify gender-stereotypical representations—often exceeding the influence of the explicit gender label itself—while prosocial traits like Openness tend to attenuate them. Furthermore, Hindi generations exhibit a stronger baseline male skew due to grammatical gender marking, whereas English displays greater personality-driven modulation.

BIAS SCORE ∈ [−1, +1]

Centroid-Based Difference of Cosines

Given male and female reference centroids cmale and cfemale in the IndicSBERT semantic space, each sentence si receives a bias score:

bias(si) = cos(si, cmale) − cos(si, cfemale)

Values range in [−1, +1], where positive scores indicate semantic alignment with male stereotypes and negative scores indicate alignment with female stereotypes. The artifact is summarized via peak salience: story(S) = bias(sk) where k = argmax |bias(si)|.

Empirical Results

Across 23,400 stories, personality traits are significantly associated with both the magnitude and direction of gender stereotyping.

OLS Personality Trait Effects (βt)
Positive values shift toward male stereotypes (p < 0.05).
TRAIT EFFECT DIRECTION & MAGNITUDE COEF (β)
← Female Attenuation Neutral (0.00) Male Amplification →
Finding 1: Amplification vs Attenuation
Dark Triad Consistently Amplifies Male Bias

Machiavellianism (β = +0.021) and Psychopathy (β = +0.018) systematically amplify male-stereotypical framing across all architectures, while traits like Openness (β = −0.006) and Emotionality (β = −0.004) attenuate it.

Finding 2: Relative Strength
Personality Frequently Exceeds Explicit Gender

In multiple model specifications, the marginal effect of assigning an antagonistic personality trait is larger in magnitude than the effect of explicit gender labeling (β = +0.011 for male personas).

Finding 3: Linguistic Heterogeneity
Hindi Morphology vs English Modulation

Hindi exhibits a stronger default male baseline across models due to grammatical verb agreement and societal defaults, whereas English exhibits wider variance and larger personality-driven shifts.

Limitations

We highlight four primary boundary conditions for the scope and methodology of this study:

Cultural Validity of Personality Models
HEXACO and Dark Triad frameworks originate from Western personality psychology and may not fully capture indigenous personality constructs within the Indian cultural context.
Lexicon Translation & Regional Nuance
Stereotype lexicons were compiled in English and translated into Hindi with native speaker validation, which may omit colloquial or dialect-specific expressions without direct English equivalents.
Peak Salience Aggregation
Our metric uses max-abs sentence aggregation to capture the most salient stereotype statement. While validated with high agreement (87.2%), alternative aggregations (e.g., mean) may capture different nuances.
Single-Turn vs. Interactive Multi-Turn Dialogue
Our study evaluates single-turn generation with explicit persona prompts. Real-world user interactions are interactive and extended, where personas may evolve or drift dynamically.
